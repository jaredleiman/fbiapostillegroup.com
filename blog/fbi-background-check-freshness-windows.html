How Fresh Must an FBI Background Check Be?

FBI Apostille Group November 2026 6 min read
Freshness is set by the receiving authority, not the FBI

An FBI Identity History Summary does not expire. There is no date printed on it after which it stops being valid, and the U.S. Department of State will apostille a report regardless of its age. And yet "your background check is too old" is one of the most common reasons an international filing stalls. Both things are true, because freshness is not set by anyone who issues the document. It is set by whoever receives it.

Whose clock it is

Consulates, employers, school districts, adoption authorities, and licensing bodies each decide how recent an FBI report must be for their filing. The window is theirs, it differs from one authority to the next, and it is rarely generous. When a filing is refused over freshness, the refusal is the receiving authority enforcing its own window — not a defect in the report and not a defect in the apostille.

Which date they count from

This is the detail that catches people. A freshness window can be measured from the date the FBI issued the report, or applied at the date of filing, with the report required to fall inside a set period before it. Those produce different deadlines. An apostille does not reset either clock: the apostille certifies the FBI's signature, and its date does not make the underlying report newer. A fresh apostille on an old report is still an old report.

How reports age out in practice

The standard failure looks like this. An applicant orders the FBI report early, because it feels like the foundational document. The rest of the packet — a diploma apostille, a marriage certificate, translations, an employer's own paperwork — takes months to assemble. By filing day, the report that started the process is the document that fails it. Nothing went wrong except the sequence.

Planning around the window

  • Ask the receiving authority, in writing where you can, how recent the FBI report must be and which date they count from. Employer templates and consulate checklists usually carry the answer; when they do not, ask before you order.
  • Work backward from the filing date. If the rest of your packet will take months, the FBI report belongs late in the sequence, not first.
  • Track the issue date across the whole packet. Other documents often carry their own freshness rules, and the filing only moves as fast as its stalest required document.
  • Keep fingerprints current too. If a report does age out, the remedy is a new FBI request — new submission, new report, new apostille. There is no renewal stamp for the old one.

The short version

The FBI report never expires, and your filing can still reject it for age. Find the receiving authority's window first, then let that window — not habit — decide when you order the report.

Frequently Asked Questions: FBI Report Freshness

Does an FBI background check expire?

No. The Identity History Summary carries no expiration date, and the Department of State will apostille it at any age. Expiration is imposed by the authority receiving your filing, under its own rules.

Does a new apostille make an older report acceptable?

No. The apostille certifies the FBI's signature; it does not change the report's issue date. A receiving authority measuring freshness from the issue date will measure the same date after the apostille as before it.

My report aged out before my filing. What is the fix?

A new FBI request, producing a newly issued report, followed by a new apostille. There is no way to refresh, extend, or re-date an existing report.
