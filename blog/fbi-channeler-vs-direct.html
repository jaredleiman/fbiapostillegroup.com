Channeler vs. Direct: What Changes on the Report You Receive

FBI Apostille Group November 2026 6 min read
The same FBI document, whichever door the request came through

There are two doors into the same federal record. You can request your FBI Identity History Summary directly from the FBI, or you can request it through an FBI-approved channeler — a private company the FBI authorizes to submit fingerprints and deliver results. Applicants ask us constantly whether the channeler route produces a "different" background check, and whether the State Department treats the two versions differently at the apostille step. It does not. Here is what actually changes, and what does not.

What does not change: the document

Both routes produce the same thing — an Identity History Summary issued by the FBI, reporting what the FBI's records hold. A channeler does not run its own background check, does not see a different database, and cannot add, remove, or soften anything on the report. It is a submission and delivery channel into the FBI's system, nothing more.

Because the issuing authority is the FBI either way, the apostille step is identical. The U.S. Department of State authenticates the FBI's document; it does not ask, and does not care, which door the request came through.

What does change: the mechanics around the report

  • How fingerprints are submitted. Channelers take fingerprints electronically at their locations and transmit them to the FBI. A direct request follows the FBI's own submission channels.
  • How the result comes back. A channeler returns the result to you electronically through its own system. A direct request returns through the FBI's channels.
  • What you pay, and to whom. A channeler charges its own service fee for the handling, on top of the federal component. A direct request carries no private intermediary's fee.
  • Who the result can go to. A channeler request is built around the individual receiving their own record. If an employer or consulate needs your report for a filing, the workable pattern is that you obtain it and provide it — plan for that hand-off rather than assuming a third party can pull it directly.

Choosing a route for an apostille filing

For apostille purposes, either route works, because the apostille attaches to the FBI's document, not to the delivery method. What matters is the condition of the report when it reaches the authentication step:

  • Keep the report as issued. If it arrives as a PDF, that file is the document — send it as issued.
  • Do not notarize a printout. Notarization does not strengthen an FBI report for State Department purposes and can force a restart with a clean copy.
  • If your record contains an error, neither a channeler nor the apostille can fix it. Record challenges run through the FBI and the state repositories that supplied the information — a separate process from authentication entirely.

The one-sentence version

Channeler versus direct is a decision about how you submit fingerprints and receive the result. The report, and everything the State Department does with it afterward, is the same.

Frequently Asked Questions: Channeler vs. Direct

Is a channeler report a different document from a direct FBI report?

No. Both are the FBI's Identity History Summary, issued by the FBI from the same records. The channeler is a submission and delivery channel, not a separate background check.

Does the State Department treat a channeler report differently for apostille?

No. The Department of State authenticates the FBI's document. The route the request traveled does not appear in, or affect, the apostille.

Can my employer order my FBI report through a channeler?

Channeler requests are designed for individuals to obtain their own record. The standard pattern for an employment filing is that you obtain the report yourself and provide it to the employer.
