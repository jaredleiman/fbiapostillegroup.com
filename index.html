LEGAL CLARITY

FBI Apostille Questions Answered Clearly

Everything you need to know about Department of State regulations, turnaround limits, and document preparation.

help What is an FBI apostille and why do I need one?

An FBI apostille is an official authentication certificate attached to your FBI Identity History Summary by the U.S. Department of State in Washington, D.C. Required by over 100 Hague Convention member nations for visas, foreign employment, adoption, and dual citizenship. Without it, foreign ministries will reject the background check as unverified.

schedule How long does the FBI apostille process take?

Standard processing takes approximately 8 to 10 weeks. Expedited priority handling takes approximately 3 to 5 weeks. These are averages based on active federal agency queues. Timelines are set by the FBI and U.S. Department of State and cannot be bypassed by any company.

gavel Can a notary or state Secretary of State apostille my FBI report?

No, absolutely not. An FBI background check is a federal government record. Only the U.S. Department of State Authentications Section in Washington, D.C. can legally apostille it. A state-level apostille or local notary stamp on an FBI report will be promptly rejected by consulates abroad.

picture_as_pdf Can I submit as a PDF or does it have to be mailed?

Both options are accepted. If you retrieved your FBI background check online through an FBI Approved Channeler or the FBI CJIS portal, you can upload the electronic PDF as issued. Do NOT print and notarize it. If you need physical fingerprint cards, we can mail cards to you.

security Is FBI Apostille Group affiliated with the government?

No. FBI Apostille Group is a private document expediting and preparation service. We are not affiliated with, endorsed by, or agents of the FBI, DOJ, or U.S. Department of State. We assist private individuals and corporations in navigating official government submission channels efficiently.

public Which countries require an FBI apostille?

More than 100 countries accept the federal apostille, including Spain, Italy, Portugal, France, Germany, Mexico, Colombia, Australia, South Korea, China, and the UK. For non-Hague nations such as the UAE, Qatar, and Kuwait, documents require federal authentication and embassy legalization instead.
